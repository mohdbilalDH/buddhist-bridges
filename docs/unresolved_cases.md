## 1. Negative results (searched, not confirmed)

### 1.1 Goryeo Hyeil (惠日) — India journey NOT verified
- Claim: a Goryeo monk named Hyeil travelled to India c. 1024.
- Result: **NOT VERIFIED.** Searches of the Goryeosa database (db.history.go.kr/goryeo) and KCI scholarship found no record of an India journey for a Goryeo Hyeil. The claim appears only in tertiary/popular retellings.
- Decision: **excluded from the dataset.** The distinct Silla Hyeil (惠日, disciple of Amoghavajra, fl. 8th c.) IS included as P-0010 with HIGH confidence.
- Sources: `goryeosa_db` (negative result), `kci_kimsooyeon`, `encykorea_milgyo`.

### 1.2 Sŏ Chŏng-ju (서정주, 1915–2000) — India link RESOLVED (2026-08-17, P5 round)
- Candidate reason: major 20th-century Korean poet; possible Asian poetry congress contacts.
- Result: **RESOLVED — no India travel, but a documented India encounter.** The Dongguk University newspaper (동대신문, reposted 2010-08-11) documents Indian Gujarati poet Sitanshu Yashaschandra's visit to Dongguk University and his meeting with Sŏ (문리대학장, ~1970s); they discussed Indian poetry and Korean-Indian literary exchange; Yashaschandra praised Sŏ's *Hwasa* for compassion and proposed mutual translation and a '자비회' society. No evidence Sŏ himself visited India.
- Decision: P-0068 reclassified SECONDARY/REVIEW → **SECONDARY/ADAPT/B5/(ENCOUNTER)**; P-0076 (Yashaschandra) and R-0021 added. Supersedes the earlier "NONE FOUND" entry.

### 1.3 Hwang Tong-gyu (황동규, b. 1938) — no India connection found
- Candidate reason: leading contemporary poet; international academic stays.
- Result: **NONE FOUND.** Documented stays are Korea, US (Iowa), UK (Edinburgh) — no India.
- Decision: included as P-0069, status=UNRESOLVED, confidence=LOW.

### 1.4 Kim Chi-ha (김지하, 1941–2022) — Gandhi link CLOSED as documented negative (2026-08-17, P5 round)
- Candidate reason: popular association of his "life philosophy" (생명사상) with Gandhi/ahimsa.
- Result: **DOCUMENTED NEGATIVE.** Hani 2026-07-03 (강성만; interview with 임진택, '김지하를 다시 본다') documents his life philosophy as Donghak-rooted (시천주/향아설위), with 증산도 개벽사상, Buddhism and Christianity — no Gandhi/India influence documented. The popular Gandhi/ahimsa association remains unverified rather than refuted.
- Decision: P-0070 reclassified MEDIATOR/REVIEW → **NONE/EXCLUDE/B5/(INDIRECT)**; status field kept UNRESOLVED to mark the unverified popular association. Do not claim a Gandhi link without a primary source.

## 2. Legendary / contested figures (included with explicit flags)

### 2.1 Heo Hwang-ok (허황옥) and King Suro (수로왕) — P-0007, P-0008
- Status: **LEGENDARY**, confidence **SPECULATIVE**.
- The Garakguk-gi (via Samguk Yusa) records the legendary marriage of King Suro of Geumgwan Gaya to Princess Heo Hwang-ok of "Ayuta" (아유타), often identified with Ayodhya.
- Scholarly debate: Kim Byung-mo's Ayodhya thesis vs. skeptics (Pankaj Mohan, Kim Tae-sik) who read "Ayuta" as a Yangtze-region polity (cf. Ko Il-hong, SNU).
- The user's existing archive `/Users/mohdbilal/Desktop/Network_Project/Heo_Hwang-ok_Archive/` contains academic literature and government-source databases relevant to this case.
- Sources: `sy_garak`, `oapen_heohwangok`, `snu_koilhong`, `wiki_suro`.

### 2.2 Tagore's "Lamp of the East" — authenticity documented
- The famous 15-line poem "동방의 등불" is a **composite/fabrication**; the authentic text is a **6-line English note** of 1929-03-28, translated into Korean by Chu Yo-han (4-line rendering, Dong-a Ilbo, Apr 1929).
- Documented in P-0030 notes and TX-0010; sources: `kyosu_tagore`, `koreatimes_tagore`, `kci_hong`, `donga_tagore`.
