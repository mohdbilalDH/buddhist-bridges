## 2. Gaps by period — v0.2 re-audit with research results

### B1 (strong — refine, don't expand broadly)
- **Gap:** Other Silla/Goguryeo monks in Yijing's compilation and in Chinese Buddhist records beyond the 8 currently captured (e.g., further names in *Da Tang Xiyu qiufa gaoseng zhuan*, Song-era compilations).
- **Gap:** Woncheuk's Indian-lineage specifics (his connection to Xuanzang's Yogacara corpus) — currently TEXTUAL; a direct Indian teacher link would upgrade the connection.
- **Gap:** Post-Hyecho 8th–9th c.: Korean monks in India after c. 750 are almost unattested — verify whether any late-Silla/early-Goryeo record exists (e.g., in *Samguk Yusa* pilgrimage lore).
- **Gap:** *Wang ocheonchukguk jeon* reception history (translations, editions) — currently one TEXT record.

### B2 (medium — one strong thread)
- **Gap:** Other Indian monks in Goryeo beyond Dhyānabhadra (e.g., 13th-c. Indian monks recorded in Goryeo sources around the Mongol period; the "Jikong circle" including Ch'anyŏng/T'aego Pu).
- **Gap:** Goryeo monks who travelled toward India/Tibet in the Mongol era (16 Goryeo monks reported at Indian sites in Yuan-era records — needs verification).
- **Gap:** Hoeamsa's Nalanda-model claim — archaeology/history exists in sources; only one INSTITUTION record.

### B3 (empty — structural, documented as a finding)
- **Structural gap:** Joseon's anti-Buddhist policy (1392–~1900) meant no documented Korea–India Buddhist travel. B3 stays an explicitly documented "no records found" band in the final narrative.
- **Possible micro-gap:** Joseon monks citing Indian texts in commentary/travelogues (e.g., Hyujŏng's *Sŏn'ga kwigam* mentions of India) — INDIRECT/TEXTUAL records if sourced. Verify via KRpia/EncyKorea before adding.

### B4 (thin — research round partially resolved, no new records yet)
- **Paek Yong-sŏng (백용성, 1864–1940) — researched, NOT confirmed.** Major reformer monk: March 1st Movement 33 national representatives (민족대표 33분), founded Daegaksa (대각사), led the 1920s anti-Japanese-seikatsu (불이) movement. **No India pilgrimage found** in available sources; his overseas activity centred on Manchuria/China. Stays a candidate (REVIEW) until a documented India link surfaces — research target: 1910s–20s Buddhist journal reports of his travels.
- **Kim Iryŏp (김일엽, 1896–1971) — researched, NOT confirmed.** Nun-poet (EncyKorea E0010283): born Pyeongannam-do Yonggang; studied in Japan; founded *New Woman* (신여자) magazine 1920; worked at Tonga Ilbo and *Pulgyo* (불교) magazine; converted to Buddhism 1928 under Man'gong; entered Sudeoksa. **No India link documented.** Her Tagore-era literary circle is CONTEXT; a documented India item would make her SECONDARY.
- **Pang Han-am** — Seon master, teacher of Pŏpchŏng; India connection only via later disciples — still REVIEW.
- **Text gap:** Colonial-era Korean Buddhist journals (e.g., *Pulgyo* magazine) carrying India/Sanskrit content; *Chosŏn Pulgyo T'ongsa* editions already in.
- **Event gap:** Korean monks visiting India in 1900–1945 remains unattested — verify before claiming.

### B5 (thin — research target)

## 5. What the gaps mean for the final product

- The **timeline** will show a genuine U-curve — good for narrative (ancient flourishing → Joseon silence → colonial re-awakening → contemporary institutional boom).
- The **network analysis** will be dominated by two clusters (B1 monastic travel network centred on Nalanda/Bodh Gaya; B6 Delhi/Bodh Gaya institutional network) with a thin textual-influence bridge across B4–B5 — the Dhyānabhadra/Hoeamsa cluster links the two eras, and the MEDIATOR edges (Tagore, Gandhi, Vinoba) thread the B4–B5 bridge.
- **GIS** will need the pilgrimage-route node set (Sarnath → Bodh Gaya → … → Shravasti) that the 2023–2026 events provide.
- The v0.2 graded scale turns the middle band from "missing people" into a nuanced story: B4 is not empty, it is a *reception* era (texts and influences, not movement) — the MEDIATOR/CONTEXTUAL grades make that visible.
